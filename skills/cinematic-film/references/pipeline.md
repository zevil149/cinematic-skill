# The documentary pipeline

For documentaries of 5, 10, 20 or 30 minutes, `docpipe.py` runs the work as stages, each in its own folder, each checked before the next starts. It is the same method as the rest of this skill; the pipeline adds a research store that every line must cite, length budgets, a storyboard, and resume.

You (the agent) do the research and the writing. The tools scaffold the files, check them, and refuse to move on while a check fails. Nothing is generated that has not been researched.

## Stages

| Stage | Folder | Tool | Done when |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| topic | `00_topic/` | `docpipe.py new` | topic and length are set |
| research | `01_research/` | `research.py` | every claim has a source; `research.md` written |
| outline | `02_outline/` | `outline.py` | every section written, budget adds up. **Gate** |
| script | `03_script/` | `narration.py` | every line passes the fact check; `script.json` exported. **Gate** |
| scenes | `04_scenes/` | `storyboard.py` | storyboard, camera moves and image list written |
| visuals | `05_visuals/` | `visuals.py` | every image need has a licensed image or clip, or a designed card. **Gate** |
| assets | `assets/prep/` | `prepare.py` | board textures, graded photos, footage frames and map data prepared for the engine |
| scenegen | `06_film/` | `scenegen.py` | an editable `scene.py` per act, and a root `scene.py` that renders one |
| voice | `audio/voice/` | `voice.py` | a segment for every line from `voice.json` (the free espeak scratch voice unless you chose another) |
| narration | `audio/` | `narrate.py` | `narration.m4a` and `timing.json` with word times; storyboard and scene starters re-timed to the voice |
| effects | `audio/` | `effects.py` | `sfx.wav` from every act's scene cues and the cue sheet's `synth_cues` (synthesised; partial without skia) |
| sound | `audio/` | `cuesheet.py`, `audiolib.py` | `cues.json` (a synthesised bed to start) and a `mix.m4a` that passed its loudness and clipping checks |
| subtitles | `out/` | `subtitles.py` | `subtitles.<lang>.srt` and `.vtt`, each cue marked voice-timed or estimated |
| render | `out/acts/` | `finish.py render` | every act rendered at its approved timing, checked; waits for the look approval |
| join | `out/film.<lang>.mp4` | `finish.py join` | the acts in order, crossfaded where they meet, the whole mix laid over; matches the timeline |
| captions | `out/film.<lang>.captions.mp4` | `finish.py captions` | the film with the edition's caption style burned in; SRT and VTT untouched |
| qc | `out/qc.<lang>.md`, `out/qc.<lang>.json`, `out/delivery.<lang>.json` | `qc.py` | the finished film checked, blocking errors apart from warnings; the delivery manifest; the edition is complete only when nothing blocks it |
| review | `07_review/<lang>/` | `review.py` | a still at every line's key word, strips of the camera moves, a contact sheet, flags and a checklist per act. **Gate: look**, per edition |

The `assets` stage (shared by every edition, at the production root) wraps `prep_assets.py`, `prep_footage.py` and `make_map.py`: photos are graded from `photos.json`, clips cut into frames from `footage.json`, and maps built from `map.json`, each only when its source changes. `assets/prep/prepared.json` records what it wrote; a prepared file you edited, or one made before this stage existed, is kept (`prepare.py --project my-doc --force` replaces it, keeping a `.bak`). Originals are only read. Map boundary data given as a URL is downloaded only with `docpipe.py run --download-maps` (public-domain data, cached in `assets/data/`), never `--offline`; a local file needs no network. A missing or unreadable image or clip, or a machine without numpy, Pillow or ffmpeg, leaves the stage `partial`: the film shows designed cards there and the next run tries again.

`run` goes on from `scenegen` through the assets, the voice, the narration timing, the effects, the mix, the subtitles and the look review; once `approve look` is given for an edition, the next `run` renders its acts, joins them, burns the captions and checks the finished film (`qc`). `run --until scenegen` stops where earlier versions stopped.

- **Paid voices are never started by `run` alone.** A paid provider in `voice.json` stops the voice stage until you run `docpipe.py run --confirm-cost` (with `--max-cost`, $5 by default), and a Hindi paid voice also needs `voice.py review --by NAME`. The free espeak and command voices need neither.
- **A failure stops the stages after it,** with the reason and, where it applies, the command that fixes it. Run again after fixing; finished stages are not redone.
- **Your files are never replaced by a run.** A segment or recording you edited is used as it is; a `narration.m4a`, `timing.json`, `mix.m4a` or subtitle file you edited (or that another tool made) stops its stage with "it is yours". Replacing it is your decision: the stage's own tool with `--force`, which keeps a `.bak` (for example `subtitles.py --project my-doc --force`), then `run` again.
- **The look review.** `review` is the last stage of each edition: `07_review/<lang>/act_NN/` holds a still at each line's key word (and the act's first and last frames), a strip of each camera move between sections and of the closing pull-back, `sheet.jpg` with every still, and `review.md`, the checklist (hero lit and readable, nothing cut by the frame edge, type never dark on dark, labels not colliding, no empty frame mid-move, the edition's own script on screen). Automated flags (blank, near-uniform, very dark) point at stills worth a second look; they never pass or fail the look. A person approves it: `docpipe.py approve --project my-doc look --lang hi`, once per edition. It comes after the audio stages, so waiting for a look never holds up a voice, a mix or the other edition; editing a scene, the timing or the assets lapses that edition's approval. Notes you write in `review.md` are kept (the next checklist goes to `review.latest.md`). `run --until subtitles` stops before it.
- **Rendering (after the look).** `render@<lang>` waits until that edition's look is approved, then renders every act through `render.py` (the engine's memory-aware parallel workers) into `out/acts/act_NN.mp4`: each act covers its own part of the timeline, from its title card (or the film's start) to the next act, plus a quarter second each side for the crossfade the join makes, and carries the matching slice of the mix. `run --size 1280x720` (default 1920x1080) and `--workers N` set the render. Each act is checked before it counts (it decodes, H.264 at the asked size, the frames its range needs); a failed act is reported, the others go on, and the stage fails rather than leave an incomplete film marked done. An act is rendered again only when its scene, the engine, the timing, the prepared assets, the size or its range changed; an act file it did not record, or that you changed, is kept unless `finish.py render --force` (with a `.bak`).
- **Joining.** `join@<lang>` puts the rendered acts together in act order (which must also be timeline order) into `out/film.<lang>.mp4`, and lays the edition's mix over the whole film once, so the sound has no seam at an act change. Where two acts meet they crossfade over half a second centred on the act change, inside the title card's silence, so the film is exactly as long as the timeline; an act of another size or frame rate is normalised to the first act's (with a warning). A film of one act is copied, not encoded again. The film counts only when its duration and frame count match the timeline, it has an audio stream and it is the right size; an act rendered from older scenes or timing is not joined; a film it did not make is kept unless `finish.py join --force` (`.bak`).
- **Burned-in captions.** `captions@<lang>` burns the edition's captions into a second file, `out/film.<lang>.captions.mp4`; the clean film stays, with `out/subtitles.<lang>.srt` and `.vtt`, for platforms that show subtitles themselves. `captions.json` in the edition chooses `{"style": "clean" | "highlighter" | "paper" | "redbar", "highlight": "auto" | "all" | "none", "enabled": true}`; `clean` (sentence case over a soft dark gradient, legible over any picture) is the default, and `"enabled": false` delivers the film with its subtitle files only. Captions follow the edition's word timing (word by word where the voice timed the words), Hindi is shaped by HarfBuzz, and no caption is on screen in a title card's silence. Captions sit in the bottom quarter of the frame: keep map labels and other text that must not be covered out of that band. The burned film must match the clean film's frames, length, size and sound; a captioned film it did not make is kept unless `finish.py captions --force` (`.bak`).
- **Quality control and delivery.** `qc@<lang>` (`qc.py`) is the last stage. It reads the finished film and changes nothing: the film and the captioned copy decode, with H.264 video and an audio stream at the rendered size and frame rate; the film is the timeline's length with every frame, sound and picture end together; the acts were joined in the order of `acts.json` with each crossfade on its act change and every act's render recorded; black frames (ffmpeg `blackdetect`) and near single-colour frames outside the film's own fade in (0.9 s) and fade out (2.2 s) and the title cards; every narration line is heard in its place; silences of a second or more where nothing pauses (the lead-in, the pauses between lines, the title cards' silence and the tail are known from the timing and never flagged); clipping (true peak above -0.1 dBTP; above -1 dBTP is a warning); the SRT and VTT exist, match `audio/subs.json`, sit on their lines in the current timing and inside the film; burned captions are visible where a line is spoken. Missing narration, black frames of a second or more, a short or incomplete film, wrong act order, missing or out-of-sync subtitles and clipping block delivery; the rest are warnings to look at. Spans you mean, such as a deliberate cut to black, go in the edition's `qc.json`: `{"allow_black": [[t0, t1]], "allow_silence": [[t0, t1]]}`. It writes `out/qc.<lang>.md` and `.json` and the delivery manifest `out/delivery.<lang>.json` (the films, subtitles, soundtrack, narration, reports and credits with their sizes and SHA-256, and the title, length, size, acts and caption style), whose `"complete"` is true only when nothing blocks. Phase 6's final report is not part of it.
- **The final report (phase 6.5).** `report` is the last stage (`report.py`), for the whole production. `reports/final_report.md` (and `report.json`) gathers each edition's status, its script check and every claim its lines cite with the claim's status, a warning where an English line shares few words with the claim it cites (other languages: numbers only, as the claims are written in English), the image needs (placed, own, designed cards), which images were picked automatically and whether a person acknowledged them, that every placed file opens, the mix (length, loudness, true peak, clipping) and the voice with what it cost, each film's QC and delivery, the trail from each claim to its sources, the lines that state it in each edition, their scenes and the images on them with their rights, and a section on what none of this proves. `reports/credits.md`, and `out/credits.<lang>.md` next to each film, list everything the films use with full attributions and links. The report is built from the records alone (no clock, no network), so the same records make the same report, and it resumes like any stage. It is complete only when every edition in the run is; otherwise the stage fails and says which edition and why. Automatic image picks: the visuals gate is not approved while an image the stage chose by itself has not been looked at: choose it in `selection.json`, or keep it with `visuals.py acknowledge --project my-doc I003`, run again and approve.
- **Rights and credits (phase 6.4).** What the film uses (the photos and clips its act scenes show, read from the scenes; the maps they draw; the music and sounds in the mix report) is judged by the documented policy before anything is rendered or delivered (`rights.py`): a free licence as the archive or entry states it passes (CC BY and BY-SA with their attribution); a licence that needs clearing passes only with `"cleared_by"` and a `"note"` (in `selection.json`, or on a `photos.json` / `footage.json` entry you wrote), recorded as that person's clearance; a refused licence never passes; your own file needs the licence and credit you state; a scene showing a file with no rights record is blocked; music and sounds must be cleared in the audio library. An act that would show something not usable is not rendered, and QC blocks the delivery; QC lists every clearance a person gave as a warning to check. Assets listed or downloaded but never shown are neither judged nor credited. `out/rights.<lang>.json` keeps every verdict with its licence, attribution, source and who cleared what, and the delivery manifest carries it; none of this proves an asset is free to use, it records what was stated and cleared. The closing credits (`credits.py`) list those assets, the map data and the narration voice under the edition's own headings (English or Hindi), drawn on the film's board as a still page or a slow roll, rendered with the acts into `out/credits.<lang>.mp4` and joined after the film's last fade; the film is then the timeline plus the credits, and QC checks both. An edition that uses nothing needing credit has no credits sequence; `credits.json` `{"enabled": false}` turns it off, with a warning to give the attributions where the film is published. Map data's licence is credited when `map.json` gives one (`"license"`, `"credit"`) and warned about when not.
- **Composition and type (phase 6.3).** A card sets its text to fit: a title too wide for the card wraps onto two balanced lines and is made smaller only if it still does not fit, long lines are made smaller, and a crowded card scales its text block; nothing goes below 55% of its size (`engine.CARD_FIT_MIN`), and what still does not fit is reported by QC. Text that fits is set exactly as before. Generated scenes frame each line's whole hero, its visuals and the typed words over them (measured in their own font, Hindi shaped, the parallax drift allowed for), inside a safe area clear of the captions at the bottom, through the push to the key word; the shot holds on the hero while its line is spoken and moves in the pause. An earlier line's item the frame would cut at its edge is left out, by a closer centred shot or by moving the shot as far as the hero stays whole. Typed words over a visual sit just above it. A line's own `"zoom"` in the scene table is kept. The fonts each role resolves to on the machine (a project `fonts/<role>.ttf` by its SHA-256, else the system family) are part of the look review's and the act renders' keys, so a different font lapses the look instead of quietly changing the film; QC warns when a role the film's text uses falls back to the system default, and reports still frames of bare board outside the fades and title cards. Look approvals and act renders made before this change are made again once, as with any engine change.
- **What QC measures (phase 6.2).** The narration is found in the film by comparing it with `audio/narration.m4a`: over the voice's own speech frames (pauses left out), after aligning the two by cross-correlation, the correlation between the narration and the film's sound gives the narration's share of the sound in dB over the music and effects, and the projection its gain. A line whose share is under -10 dB is missing or buried (blocking); under +3 dB masked, or mixed 10 dB quieter than the other lines, is a warning; a line simply spoken quietly keeps its share and is not flagged. Music alone can no longer pass for narration. The film's integrated loudness is measured against the mix report's target (1 LU: warning, 2 LU: blocking). The SRT and VTT are parsed cue by cue (malformed blocks named; a subtitle line that is only a number is text) and compared with `audio/subs.json` in count and times. From the scenes' geometry (`layoutcheck.py`), card titles and lines wider or taller than their card, and measured text (cards, big type, counters) under an opaque burned caption, are warnings; text of other items (pin labels, stamps, ledgers) is not measured, and the report says how many. Anything that cannot be checked is listed under "Not checked" with the reason; `qc.json` keeps every measurement (loudness, each line's narration share and gain, overflows, collisions).
- **Resume.** Each stage records a hash of the files it reads and writes. The run after a new voice re-checks the storyboard and scene starters once, because they now follow the voice; nothing is regenerated that did not change. A new voice never reopens the approved visuals: their gate counts the images asked for, not the storyboard's timing.

```bash
python3 scripts/docpipe.py new my-doc --topic "The 1919 Boston molasses flood" --minutes 10
python3 scripts/research.py add-source --project my-doc --url https://... --title "..." --publisher "..." --type archive
python3 scripts/research.py add-claim --project my-doc --text "..." --source S1 --source S2
python3 scripts/docpipe.py run --project my-doc        # writes the outline skeleton, stops at its TODOs
python3 scripts/docpipe.py approve --project my-doc outline
python3 scripts/docpipe.py run --project my-doc        # narration skeleton, then the fact check
python3 scripts/narration.py spoken --project my-doc   # numbers and dates as words
python3 scripts/docpipe.py approve --project my-doc script
python3 scripts/docpipe.py run --project my-doc        # storyboard, then the visuals (approve them), scene starters,
python3 scripts/docpipe.py approve --project my-doc visuals
python3 scripts/docpipe.py run --project my-doc        # voice, narration timing, mix, subtitles
python3 scripts/docpipe.py status --project my-doc
```

`run` skips every stage whose inputs have not changed, so run it as often as you like; an interrupted job resumes at the first stage that is out of date. Editing a file reopens its stage and every stage after it, and an approval lapses when its stage's files change.

- **Gates hold under every kind of run.** The finishing stages (`render`, `join`, `captions`, `qc`) run only when the edition's look is approved for the review's current inputs: edit a scene, the timing, the storyboard or the prepared assets after `approve look` and they wait (`waits for the look ... no longer matches`) until a run makes the review again and you approve it. `run --only <stage>` does only what a full run would have reached: every earlier stage of that edition (and the shared ones) must have run successfully, and every earlier gate (outline, scripts, visuals) must be approved for its current files; otherwise it says what is missing and runs nothing.
- **State is saved as the run goes.** `docpipe.json` is written around every stage: a stage at work is `running`, one that raised or was interrupted (Ctrl-C) is `interrupted` with the reason, and the stages before it keep `done`. A killed run leaves `running`, which is never taken for `done`: the next `run` redoes that stage and skips the ones that finished.
- **Long passes have a time limit.** Each act render, the join and the caption burn run in their own process group, stopped (parallel workers and encoders included) if they run past 15 minutes plus a minute per second of film at 1080p, scaled by pixels (the join a fifth of that). The partial file is removed, nothing is recorded, and the stage fails; set `CINEMATIC_RENDER_TIMEOUT` (seconds) for a slower machine. Ctrl-C stops the group too.

## Research

- One source per entry in `01_research/sources.json`: a url (with the date you read it) or, for print, a title with an author or publisher. `research.py add-source` fills the id and accessed date.
- One claim per fact the film may state, in `claims.json`, citing source ids. Put the supporting sentence in `quote` when you have it: it is what you check the line against later.
- **Tiers.** 1 is primary or institutional (archives, museums, government, universities, journals). 2 is secondary (books, reputable news, reference works). 3 is leads only (Wikipedia, wikis, social media, blogs). A tier 3 source is never enough on its own.
- **Status.** A claim with a number or date needs a tier 1 source or two independent tier 2 sources, or it is `single-source`. Mark a claim `disputed` when sources disagree; the narration must then hedge it. A claim with no valid source is `unsupported` and no line may cite it.
- Never fill a gap with a guess. If the sources do not say it, the film does not say it.

## Outline

`outline.py init` sizes the skeleton from the length: about 150 words a minute, a cold open, one bridge, chapters (5 at 5 min, 8 at 10, 14 at 20, 21 at 30), a turn and a close. 20 and 30 minute films are split into 3 or 4 acts. `outline.py plan --minutes 20` prints the budget.

Write each section's `title`, `purpose` (what the viewer learns or feels), `period` (a year, for the chronology check), `beats` and `claims`. Move time between sections freely; the total must stay within 10% of the length.

## Script

`narration.json` holds the lines: `text`, `claims`, `visual` (the direction), `hero`, `typed`, optional `images` and `gap_after`. Line ids are global and never reused. The check:

- a line cites at least one claim, unless it is `"kind": "framing"` (a line that states no fact: a question, a transition, a reflection)
- every number in the text appears in a cited claim (so "59 seconds" cannot slip in against a claim that says 12)
- a disputed claim is hedged, and weak or single-source claims are flagged for the review
- 4 to 22 words a line, a hero, a visual direction, and each section within 25% of its word budget

`narration.py spoken` writes each line's `spoken` text: "December 17th, 1903" becomes "December seventeenth, nineteen oh three", "$50,000" becomes "fifty thousand dollars". Fix names the voice will stumble on by hand. Export writes `script.json` for the film tools, with each claim and its source urls in `fact_notes`, and never replaces a `script.json` it did not write.

## Storyboard

`storyboard.py` gives every line a scene: start and end time, narration, hero and visual, typed text, the key word to time the hit on (`doc.W(line, "word")`), a suggested camera move, and the images it needs. Times are estimates until `audio/timing.json` exists; run it again after each voice (in an edition, `narrate.py` builds the timing from the voice segments and re-runs the storyboard and scene starters itself). `04_scenes/image_needs.json` lists every image with a search query, for `fetch_commons.py`.

## Visuals

`visuals.py` reads `04_scenes/image_needs.json` and, for each need, asks the archives in order (`topic.json` `"archives"`, default Commons, Library of Congress, Openverse, then Smithsonian and Europeana when their keys are set), stopping at the first that offers enough free candidates. Footage needs (`"kind": "footage"`) search for video (Commons, Europeana).

- **Licences** are read from what each archive states. Free (public domain, CC0, CC BY, CC BY-SA) is used with attribution; "no known restrictions" and similar need a person to clear it; NC, ND, in copyright or no licence at all is refused. Never write a licence an archive did not state.
- **Choosing.** The best-scoring free candidate is picked. Look at `05_visuals/visuals.md` and the candidates, and override in `05_visuals/selection.json`: a candidate id, `"fallback"`, or `{"candidate": id, "cleared_by": "name", "note": "why it is clear"}` for a licence that needs clearing. The stage is a gate: approve it once the picks are right. A changed pick replaces the downloaded file (each download records the candidate it came from), and a need that names a Commons file (`"commons": "File:..."`) gets exactly that file or a fallback, never a search result in its place.
- **Your own files.** A need with `"kind": "own"`, `"file"`, `"license"` and `"credit"` uses `assets/photos/<file>`, with the rights as stated. Without a licence and credit it is not used.
- **Fallbacks.** Anything without a usable image (no free candidate, a failed download, an archive that is rate-limiting us) is drawn as a designed card with the need's description, never a stand-in photo. `visuals.md` lists every fallback so it can be replaced.
- **Rate limits.** Requests to each archive are paced and searches are cached. An archive that refuses us is left alone until its cooldown ends (`visuals.py hosts`), and the stage stays `partial` until the deferred downloads are done. `--offline` makes no requests at all.
- `photos.json` and `footage.json` get the chosen files with credit and licence (entries you wrote are kept), and `05_visuals/credits.md` lists every attribution.

Archive keys (`SI_API_KEY`, `EUROPEANA_API_KEY`) come from the environment only.

## Scene starters

`scenegen.py` writes `06_film/act_NN/scene.py` for every act: a table of the act's lines (section, key word, typed text, visuals) and `build_film(doc)`, which lays the act out as one cluster per section, puts each visual in a print (with a slow move inside the frame), plays footage in a film frame, floats typed text on a parallax plane, and keys the camera onto each hero and in to its key word, ending on a pull-back over the whole act. Lines with nothing of their own to show hold on what is there.

It is a starter: edit the table (positions, moves, sizes) or replace `build_film` with your own engine calls, then run the look test as usual. `scenegen` never overwrites a scene you have edited, nor a root `scene.py` it did not write. Render an act with `CINEMATIC_ACT=2 python3 scripts/render.py ...`; `06_film/acts.json` gives each act's time range.

## Languages and editions

One production can be told in several languages. `docpipe.py new my-doc --topic "..." --editions en,hi` makes the shared production (topic, research, outline, beats, visuals) and one edition per language in `editions/<lang>/`. The first is the default; English is the default for the main channel.

- **Each edition is a complete film project.** It reads the shared stages from the production and keeps its own `03_script`, `04_scenes`, `06_film`, `audio` and `out`, with links to the shared `assets`, `photos.json` and `footage.json`. Run any film tool on `--project my-doc/editions/hi`.
- **Beats** (`03_beats/beats.json`) are what every edition tells: one hero each, with its claims and images. `beats.py` builds them from the default edition's approved narration (a line is its own beat `B<id>` unless it names one). Every other edition starts from the beats: one line per beat, written in its own language, never copied. All editions share the same images and camera plan; each is timed by its own voice.
- **Languages never mix.** An English line may not contain Devanagari, and a Hindi line may not contain Latin words unless its `lexicon.json` lists them under `"allow"` (technical terms such as `NASA`). Typed text and image card titles are per edition: a Hindi line gives its own typed text, and every image needs a Hindi card title (`"labels": {"hi": "..."}` in `beats.json`). Every edition file is tagged with its language, and a stage refuses a file from another edition.
- **Gates are per edition:** `docpipe.py approve --project my-doc script --lang hi`. `run --lang hi` runs that edition, `--lang all` every edition; editing one edition never reopens another.
- **The order with editions:** `topic`, `research`, `outline` [gate], `script@en` [gate], `beats`, `script@hi` [gate], `scenes@en`, `scenes@hi`, `visuals` [gate], `assets` (shared), `scenegen@en`, `scenegen@hi`, then for each edition `voice@<lang>`, `narration@<lang>`, `effects@<lang>`, `sound@<lang>`, `subtitles@<lang>`, and last `review@<lang>` for each edition [gate: look]. Each edition's voice, timing, mix and subtitles stay in its folder and are tagged with its language. A typical two-language run:

  ```bash
  python3 scripts/docpipe.py run --project my-doc --lang all            # stops at each gate in turn
  python3 scripts/docpipe.py approve --project my-doc script --lang hi
  python3 scripts/voice.py review --project my-doc/editions/hi --by "Reviewer"   # before a paid Hindi voice
  python3 scripts/docpipe.py run --project my-doc --lang all --confirm-cost     # only if a voice.json names a paid voice
  python3 scripts/docpipe.py status --project my-doc
  ```
- **Language plugins** live in `scripts/lang/` (English, Hindi). English uses the same rules as a single-language project.
- **Hindi** (`lang/hi.py`): `narration.py spoken` turns numbers into Hindi words with the Indian scale (`17 दिसंबर 1903` becomes `सत्रह दिसंबर उन्नीस सौ तीन`, `₹1,00,000` becomes `एक लाख रुपये`), including percentages, decimals, ordinals (`21वीं`) and Devanagari digits. Timing counts syllables at the edition's pace (`"wpm"` in `edition.json`, 135 by default), and key words skip pronouns and postpositions. The fact check reads Hindi numbers in digits or words (`बारह`, `डेढ़ सौ`, `साढ़े तीन`) and compares them with the claims; a claim may carry `"text_hi"` too. The number words follow standard Hindi; the native-speaker review of the script is where a regional preference is corrected.
- **Voice per edition:** each edition has its own `voice.json` and makes its own segments with `voice.py` (see `references/voice.md`); an edition never gets another language's voice. ElevenLabs is the English voice and Sarvam the Hindi one, both paid and behind the spend guard, chosen by `voice.py audition`.
- **Music and effects per edition:** one rights-gated library for the production (`audiolib.py`), and a cue sheet and mix per edition on its own timing (`cuesheet.py`); see `references/sound.md`.
- **Hindi on screen:** typed text, cards, labels and captions draw Devanagari correctly (HarfBuzz shaping, bundled Noto Sans Devanagari); see `references/film.md`.
- **Subtitles per edition:** `subtitles.py --project my-doc/editions/hi` writes `out/subtitles.hi.srt` and `out/subtitles.hi.vtt` (UTF-8, WebVTT with a note of how many cues are on estimated times) and `audio/subs.json` (every cue with its line, timing source and reading speed) from the edition's `audio/timing.json`, which it only reads. Cues show the script's text, cut at sentence ends (. ! ? ।), then clauses, then length: at most 2 lines of 42 characters and 7 seconds, lines broken evenly and after punctuation where possible, held at least 1 s and about 17 characters a second for reading, never overlapping (80 ms apart) and never crossing from one narration line into the next. A cue is labelled `voice`, `voice-approx` or `estimate` by its words (`references/voice.md`). The same timing makes nothing new; a subtitle file it did not write, or that you edited, is never replaced without `--force` (`.bak` kept). `editions.py check` refuses subtitles named for another language or written in another script. `burn_captions.py --srt-only` still writes the single-film SRT as before.
- **Lexicon** (`editions/<lang>/lexicon.json`): give every English name, place or term its pronunciation in the edition's language: `python3 scripts/editions.py lexicon --project my-doc hi --add "Kitty Hawk" "किटी हॉक"`. The spoken text uses it and the displayed text keeps what you wrote. A term that may stay in Latin in a Hindi line also goes under `"allow"` (`--allow NASA`, with `--add NASA नासा` for how it is said). `editions.py check` refuses a pronunciation written in the wrong script.

A project made without `--editions` is a single English edition at its root, exactly as before.

## Strings between clusters

Tie two moments of the story with a red string by saying so: a narration line's `"links": [3, 12]` names the lines it is tied to. Nothing is linked on its own: not by shared claims, people or words.

- **Single-language:** the storyboard keeps each line's links (unknown lines and a line linked to itself are dropped with a warning).
- **Editions:** write the links in the default edition's narration; `beats.py` turns them into links between beats, and every edition draws the same strings between the lines that tell those beats.
- **On the board:** `scenegen.py` passes `links` into the scene table (only where there are links), and `scenekit.add_strings` pulls a red `StringLine` from the linked line's visual to the linking line's as the linking line is spoken. The strings stay, so the act's closing pull-back shows them together. A link to a line in another act is left out with a warning (each act is its own board). Edit `links` in an act's `LINES` like anything else.

## Maps and routes

A line that happens somewhere can say where: `"place": "Kitty Hawk"` (and `"map": "east"` when the place is on more than one map). The place must be in a map of `map.json` (`"places": {"Kitty Hawk": [36.06, -75.70]}`); places are never looked up or guessed from the narration.

- **Data.** The `assets` stage builds the map from `map.json` (a local boundary file, or one downloaded once with `docpipe.py run --download-maps`).
- **Labels per edition.** `map.json` gives each place its label per language: `"labels": {"Kitty Hawk": {"hi": "किटी हॉक"}}`. English uses the place name; another language uses its label, then the edition lexicon's spelling of the name, else the pin has no label (with a warning). Labels are checked for the edition's script.
- **Editions.** Places are written in the default edition's narration; `beats.py` carries them on the beats, so every edition pins the same places on its own lines.
- **On the board.** `scenegen.py` passes `place` into the scene table (only where there is one). `scenekit.place_maps` lays each map used as a sheet of its own after the act's section clusters, drops a labelled pin as the line begins, puts the line's visuals beside the pin, and draws a route from the previous place on the same map. A map not prepared yet, or a place it does not have, leaves the line in its usual slot. Change `place` (or set `pos`) in an act's `LINES` like anything else.

## Long films

### Title cards between acts

A film of several acts (over 10 minutes: 3 acts up to 20 minutes, 4 beyond) opens every act after the first on a title card, held in about 2.5 seconds of silence:

- **Timing.** `narrate.py` holds `ACT_GAP` (2.5 s, or the line's own `gap_after` if longer) before the first line of each later act; the storyboard estimates the same pause before a voice exists. Subtitles have no cue in the silence. A film of one act is exactly as before.
- **Titles.** Each edition's card is in its own language. In order: the edition's `03_script/narration.json` `"act_titles": {"2": "...", "3": "..."}`; for English, the outline's optional `"act_titles"`; for English, the act's first section title. Another language must give its own titles (the scene starters stop with the reason until it does); Latin words in a Hindi title are allowed when the edition's lexicon allows them and only warned about otherwise.
- **On the board.** `scenegen.py` writes `ACT = {"act", "title", "label", "hold"}` into the act's `scene.py` (label `ACT 2` in English, `भाग 2` in Hindi) and opens the act's board 2.5 s earlier (`acts.json` `t0` and `title_card`). `scenekit.title_card` draws the label and title (Devanagari shaped) beside the act's first cluster, holds the camera on it, and moves on as the first line begins. Edit or delete `ACT` in the scene like anything else; an edited scene is never regenerated.
- **Review.** The look review adds a still of each title card (`probe_title`).

A 20 or 30 minute film is too big for one board and one review. Build, probe and draft it one act at a time, and look at each act's draft before starting the next.
