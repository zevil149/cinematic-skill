# Changelog

## Unreleased

- **Music and effects per edition (phase 3, milestone 3.6).** `audiolib.py` keeps the production's audio library: files you provide, copied in with their SHA-256 (originals never touched), each with source, licence (the same rules as images), attribution and clearance. Own work and CC0, public domain, CC BY and BY-SA files are usable (attribution recorded); NC, ND, unstated or unclear licences are refused; licensed tracks need a licence reference and your clearance; Freesound sounds need their page's licence verified (`verify --online`, metadata only) or your clearance. Nothing is downloaded, and a changed file is refused. `cuesheet.py` gives each edition its own `audio/cues.json` (music, library sounds and gaps on that edition's lines, words and sections, with fades, gain and ducking), a synthesised music bed, and a mix to -15 LUFS and -1.5 dBTP with a limiter, checked for loudness, true peak, clipping, length and the narration's margin over the music before it is written; `mix_report.json` and `music_credits.md` record every asset used. Resumable; narration, library files and effects are only read; a mix it did not make is never replaced without `--force`. `mix_soundtrack.py` is unchanged.
- **Timing per edition (phase 3, milestone 3.5).** `narrate.py` joins an edition's voice segments (manual recordings first, your edited segments as they are, all only read) into `audio/narration.m4a` and `audio/timing.json`, with silence trimmed and the script's pacing, an optional `--target` length, and per-line word times in film seconds (from the voice where it timed them, from the language's rules otherwise) and the edition's `lang`. The storyboard and scene starters are re-timed to the voice (edited scenes kept), and `doc.W` lands on the word's own start when word times exist. Runs resume (cached parts, an input hash); a track or timing it did not make is never replaced without `--force` (`.bak` kept); another language's timing is refused; missing or stale lines point to `voice.py synth` and nothing is bought. Timing files without words, and `build_narration.py`, are unchanged.
- **ElevenLabs and Sarvam voices (phase 3, milestone 3.4).** `voices.py` adds `elevenlabs` for the English edition (character timestamps, so whole sections are read and cut by word; neighbouring lines as context in line mode) and `sarvam` (Bulbul) for the Hindi edition (line by line, `hi-IN`). Model, voice or speaker, settings, output format, language and request length are checked before anything is sent; each provider refuses the other language. Keys come from `ELEVENLABS_API_KEY` and `SARVAM_API_KEY` only, at the moment of a request, and never reach a URL, a file, a log or an error (echoed keys are redacted; a key in `voice.json` is refused). Rate limits and server errors are retried with `Retry-After`; refusals, timeouts and network failures are not, so nothing is billed twice. Cost estimates in dollars (Sarvam in rupees at your `inr_per_usd`), the $5 cap and `--confirm-cost` cover synthesis and the new `voice.py audition`; `spend.json` records rupees and request ids per edition. `voice.py verify` checks a voice offline, or with `--online` by a free lookup. Tested only against a fake network (`tests/test_voice_api.py`); no live or paid request has been made.
- **Voice framework (phase 3, milestone 3.3).** `voice.py` makes an edition's narration as one WAV segment per line, through providers behind one interface (`voices.py`: espeak scratch voices for English and Hindi, any local command, a test mock). A manifest records each segment's request hash, file hash, duration, levels and word timings; runs resume, edited segments are locked (`--force` keeps a `.bak`), manual recordings always win, and validation flags missing, invalid, silent, clipped, stale and odd-length segments. Voice config, lexicon and audio stay inside their edition and language. Providers with word timings read whole sections and are cut into lines. A spend guard (estimate, $5 cap, `--confirm-cost`, native-speaker review for Hindi) is in place for the paid providers of the next milestone.
- **Devanagari rendering (phase 3, milestone 3.2).** Hindi text is shaped by HarfBuzz (`uharfbuzz`, Apache 2.0) and drawn in the bundled Noto Sans Devanagari (SIL OFL 1.1, `assets/fonts/`, SHA-256 recorded): vowel signs before their consonant, conjuncts, half forms, reph, nukta and marks come out right in `BigText`, `Card`, `Stamp`, labels and all four caption styles, with role weights from the variable font. Mixed Hindi and Latin keeps each script's font. English rendering is pixel-identical; it never takes the new path.
- **Hindi language foundation (phase 3, milestone 3.1).** Hindi numbers, years, dates, money (₹, $, €, £), percentages, decimals, ordinals and Devanagari digits become Hindi words with the Indian scale (हज़ार, लाख, करोड़, अरब). Hindi lines are timed by syllables at the edition's pace and never come out at zero; key words skip pronouns and postpositions; the camera's word timing works for Hindi lines. Pronunciation lexicons per edition (`editions.py lexicon`), applied to spoken text and checked for the right script. The fact check reads Hindi numbers in digits or words, including डेढ़, ढाई and साढ़े, against the claims. English is unchanged, byte for byte.
- **Language editions (phase 3, milestone 3.0).** One production, several languages: `docpipe.py new --editions en,hi` shares the topic, research, outline, beats and visuals, and gives each language its own `editions/<lang>/` folder (a complete film project) with its own script, scenes, film and audio. English is the default edition. `beats.py` builds the language-neutral beats every edition tells; `editions.py` adds and checks editions; `scripts/lang/` holds the English and Hindi plugins. Languages never mix: writing-system checks per line, typed text and card titles per edition, and every edition file tagged with its language. Single-language projects are unchanged, byte for byte.
- **Visual engine (documentary pipeline, phase 2).** `visuals.py` and `archives.py` find, license and fetch an image or clip for every storyboard scene across Wikimedia Commons, the Library of Congress, Openverse, Smithsonian Open Access and Europeana: licences only as the archive states them (free, needs clearing, refused), attribution recorded, polite rate limiting with cooldowns remembered across runs, resumable downloads, and designed fallback cards. Engine: `Print(move=ken_burns(...))`, `Footage` (archive clips in a print frame, via `prep_footage.py`), `Parallax`, and camera moves (`push_in`, `pull_back`, `pan`, `whip`, `reveal`, `hold`); existing items render unchanged. `scenegen.py` writes an editable `scene.py` per act (`scenekit.py`). New docpipe stages `visuals` (gate) and `scenegen`. Tests never reach the network (`CINEMATIC_OFFLINE=1`).
- **Documentary pipeline, phase 1.** `docpipe.py` runs a documentary as stages, each in its own folder, with checks, approval gates and resume: topic, research (`research.py`: sources with reliability tiers, claims tied to them, weak and unsupported claims flagged), outline (`outline.py`: 5, 10, 20 and 30 minute budgets, chronology), script (`narration.py`: every line fact-checked against the claims it cites, numbers and dates written as words, exported to `script.json`) and scenes (`storyboard.py`: timestamps, camera moves, image needs). `tests/test_docpipe.py` covers every stage and runs in CI. See `references/pipeline.md` and `docs/pipeline-plan.md`.
- `fetch_commons.py` survives Wikimedia rate limits: it retries 429 and 503 with `Retry-After` or exponential backoff, saves `photos.json` after every photo so an interrupted `get` resumes where it stopped, and writes each download through a temporary file.

## 1.0.1 (2026-11-09)

- `get_fonts.py` fetches open-licensed fonts (Bebas Neue, Homemade Apple) for text roles that Linux and Windows cannot match, so labels, stamps and handwriting keep their look. `check_setup.py` points to it.

## 1.0.0 (2026-11-09)

First release: `cinematic-film`, narrated cinematic films drawn entirely in code, on any subject.

- **Engine.** One continuous camera with motion blur and handheld drift, a spotlight, shakes and auto sound cues.
  - Items: prints and torn clippings, typeset documents, cards, big type, counters, stamps, pen marks, string, maps with region fills, routes and markers.
  - Seven looks.
- **Voice.** Scratch voice for timing, any-TTS take splitting with flagged cuts, and a narration track with a timing file.
- **Story.** Beat table with a chronology lint.
- **Images.** Wikimedia Commons fetcher that records licences and refuses non-free files; a map builder for any country; one-look grading.
- **Sound and finish.**
  - Synthesised sound effects.
  - A ducked, loudness-normalised mix.
  - A memory-aware parallel render.
  - Four caption styles and SRT.
- **Projects.** "Twelve Seconds", a 90 second documentary from seven public-domain photos, and a starter explainer.
